Age-Appropriate Chores for Kids: A List by Age

By FridgePaper · Updated November 2026 · 6 minute read

Kids can do more than most of us expect. The trick is matching the job to the age, showing them how, and accepting that the first few tries won't be perfect. A 4 year old who folds towels badly is still learning to fold towels.

Use the lists below as a starting point. Every child is different, so move up or down a group as needed. Each age group builds on the one before it, so a 10 year old can still do everything on the younger lists.

Ages 2 to 3

At this age, chores are really "helping." Keep jobs to one step and do them together.

  • Put toys in a bin
  • Put dirty clothes in the hamper
  • Carry their plate or cup to the counter
  • Help feed a pet (you measure, they pour)
  • Wipe up small spills with a cloth
  • Put shoes by the door

Ages 4 to 5

  • Make their bed (pull up the covers, put the pillow on top)
  • Set the table with napkins and silverware
  • Match socks from the clean laundry
  • Water plants with a small watering can
  • Dust low shelves with a sock on their hand
  • Help put groceries away in low cupboards

Ages 6 to 8

Kids this age can handle two or three step jobs and start doing them without being asked, if the job is on a chart.

  • Fold and put away their own clothes
  • Empty small trash cans into the big one
  • Sweep the kitchen floor
  • Unload the silverware from the dishwasher
  • Pack their own school snack
  • Feed pets on their own
  • Wipe the bathroom sink and counter

Ages 9 to 12

  • Load and unload the whole dishwasher
  • Vacuum a room
  • Take trash and recycling to the curb
  • Clean the bathroom (toilet, sink, mirror)
  • Make a simple breakfast or lunch
  • Rake leaves or pull weeds
  • Run a load of laundry with a little help

Ages 13 to 16

Teens can do almost any household job. The goal now is getting them ready to live on their own.

  • Do all of their own laundry
  • Cook a family dinner once a week
  • Mow the lawn or shovel snow
  • Clean the kitchen after a meal
  • Help with the grocery list and shopping
  • Babysit younger siblings for short stretches

The Teen Chore Chart has check boxes and a line to track money earned.

How to teach a new chore

  1. Do it while they watch.
  2. Do it together.
  3. Let them do it while you watch.
  4. Let them do it alone.

Spend a few days on each step for younger kids. It feels slow, but it saves months of re-doing jobs and nagging.

Should you pay for chores?

Families split on this. A common middle ground: everyday jobs (making your bed, clearing your plate) are just part of living in the house, and bigger extra jobs can earn money. If you go that way, the Allowance Calculator can help you land on a fair amount.
