How to Survive Christmas Break With Kids at Home: A Simple Daily Plan

By FridgePaper · Updated November 2026 · 6 minute read

The first two days of Christmas break feel like a vacation. By day four, the kids are bouncing off the walls, the screens have been on since breakfast, and every room looks like a snow globe got shaken in it. You don't need a strict schedule to fix that. You need a simple rhythm that everyone can see.

Here's a plan that keeps break fun and relaxed, without the chaos.

1. Keep the mornings the same

Bedtimes can slide a little over break, but try to keep wake-up within an hour of the school-day time. Then follow the same first steps every day: get dressed, brush teeth, eat breakfast, make the bed. When mornings are predictable, the rest of the day goes smoother, and the return to school in January is much easier.

2. Give the day a loose shape

Kids don't need every minute planned. They do better with a few anchors: chores in the morning, outside time before lunch, a quiet time after lunch, screens in the afternoon, and a family activity before dinner. Write it down and put it on the fridge so you're not the one repeating it all day.

The free Christmas Break Daily Schedule has 13 colorful time blocks from wake-up to bedtime. The times are just a guide. Cross any out and write your own.

3. Use a "before screens" rule

Instead of arguing about screen time all day, set one simple rule: screens come after the basics are done. Dressed, bed made, morning chores finished, some time outside or moving, and 20 minutes of reading. Once the list is checked off, screens are earned, not begged for. Kids like knowing exactly what it takes.

4. Turn chores into a game

Break is a great time for kids to help more, because there's no homework competing for their time. Keep it to a few small jobs a day and make progress visible. The Winter Break Chore Chart is a snowy board game: kids color one square for each chore and race toward a winter prize you pick together, like sledding with a friend or choosing the next family movie.

5. Get outside every day, even when it's cold

Fresh air resets everyone's mood, including yours. Twenty minutes is enough: build a snow fort, go on a winter walk to look for animal tracks, or let them help shovel. Bundle up, then reward everyone with hot cocoa afterward.

6. Keep a boredom buster list ready

When you hear "I'm bored," point to a list instead of solving it yourself. A few ideas that work for most ages:

  • Build a blanket fort and read inside it
  • Bake and decorate cookies
  • Make paper snowflakes for the windows
  • Start a big family puzzle on the table
  • Write thank-you notes for gifts
  • Set up an indoor scavenger hunt
  • Hold a board game tournament
  • Have a hot cocoa taste test

7. Plan one thing to look forward to

A movie night, a trip to see holiday lights, a day at the sledding hill. Write it on the calendar so kids can count down. Small, simple plans make break feel special without wearing you out.

8. End break by looking ahead

The last few days of break are a perfect time to set goals together for the new year. Kids love having a say. The free New Year 2027 Family Goals planner has a spot for your family word of the year, a goal for each person, and one family adventure for every month.
