12 Ways to Lower Your Family's Grocery Bill

By FridgePaper · Updated November 2026 · 6 minute read

Groceries are one of the few big bills you can change this week. You don't need extreme couponing. These twelve habits add up, and most of them take only a few minutes.

Before you go

1. Know your number

Decide what you want to spend each week before you shop. The free Grocery Budget Calculator turns a monthly budget into a weekly amount and a cost per meal.

2. Plan meals around what you have

Shop your own fridge and freezer first. Planning two meals a week around food you already own can cut the list a lot. See How to Meal Plan in 30 Minutes a Week.

3. Check the weekly ad

Build a meal or two around what's on sale, especially meat and produce.

4. Write a list and stick to it

Impulse buys are where budgets break. Use a list sorted by store section, like the free Grocery List, so you're in and out.

At the store

5. Try the store brand

For basics like flour, sugar, canned goods, cereal and frozen vegetables, store brands are often hard to tell apart from name brands.

6. Compare the unit price

The small print on the shelf tag shows the price per ounce or per count. The bigger package isn't always cheaper.

7. Buy whole, not cut

Whole carrots, block cheese and whole chickens usually cost less than pre-cut, shredded or boneless versions.

8. Shop alone when you can

Fewer people in the cart means fewer "can we get this?" moments. Online pickup works the same way, since you only add what's on the list.

At home

9. Eat the leftovers

Plan a leftovers night every week. Food you throw away is money you threw away.

10. Use your freezer

Freeze bread, meat, shredded cheese and ripe bananas before they go bad.

11. Make a few things from scratch

Pancake mix, taco seasoning, salad dressing and granola are cheap and quick to make at home.

12. Track it for one month

Save your receipts or jot each trip's total on the fridge. Seeing the real number for a month is often the push that changes habits.
