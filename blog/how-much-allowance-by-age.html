How Much Allowance Should a Kid Get? A Simple Guide by Age

By FridgePaper · Updated November 2026 · 5 minute read

There's no single right amount of allowance. It depends on your budget and what you expect your kids to pay for. But you need a starting number, and the most common one is easy to remember.

The simple rule: $1 per year of age, per week

Many parents use one dollar per year of age each week. A 6 year old gets $6, a 10 year old gets $10. Some families go with half that, 50 cents per year of age, especially for younger kids or when money is tight.

The rule works because it grows as your kids grow, and it's easy to explain. When a younger sibling asks why they get less, the answer is simple: you will get more on your next birthday.

Decide what the allowance has to cover

This matters more than the amount. Ten dollars a week is a lot if it's only for candy, and very little if it has to cover school lunches and clothes.

  • Ages 5 to 8: small treats, toys, saving up for something bigger.
  • Ages 9 to 12: add things like outings with friends, gifts for family, and app or game purchases.
  • Teens: some parents add clothing, gas or phone costs and raise the allowance to match.

Write down what's covered so there's no arguing later.

Split it into save, spend and give

Splitting the money into three jars helps kids see where it goes. A common split is:

  • Save: 30 to 50 percent, for bigger goals
  • Spend: 40 to 60 percent, theirs to use however they want
  • Give: about 10 percent, for a cause or someone they choose

Let them make small mistakes with the spend jar. Spending it all on something that breaks in a day is a cheap lesson at 8 years old.

Should allowance depend on chores?

There are three common approaches:

  1. Not tied to chores. Allowance is for learning to handle money. Chores are part of being in the family.
  2. Tied to chores. Do the list, get paid. Skip it, lose part of it.
  3. A mix. A small base allowance, plus extra money for bigger jobs.

The mix works for a lot of families because kids always have some money to practice with, and they can earn more. The Teen Chore Chart has a line to track what they've earned each week.

Tips that make allowance work

  • Pay on the same day every week. Kids notice when you forget.
  • Pay in cash for younger kids so they can see and count it.
  • Don't lend against next week's allowance. Waiting is part of the lesson.
  • Raise it on birthdays, not when they ask.
