How to Make a Chore Chart That Actually Works

By FridgePaper · Updated November 2026 · 5 minute read

Most chore charts follow the same story. Week one, everyone is excited. Week two, a few stars get missed. By week three the chart is buried under a pizza coupon. Here's how to make one that lasts.

1. Start with fewer chores than you think

Three to five chores is plenty. A chart with twelve jobs looks impressive and feels impossible. Once the first few are a habit, add one more.

2. Make every chore something you can see

"Clean your room" means different things to a parent and a 7 year old. "Toys in bins, clothes in hamper, bed made" is clear. If you can't check it in ten seconds, break it into smaller jobs.

3. Put it at their eye level

The fridge is popular for a reason: everyone walks by it many times a day. Put the chart low enough that your child can reach it and mark it without help.

4. Let them mark it themselves

Coloring the star or checking the box is half the reward. If a parent does the marking, it turns into the parent's chart.

5. Pick the reward together

Kids work harder for something they chose. Agree on it before the week starts and write it on the chart. Small, quick rewards work better than big, far-off ones, especially for kids under 8.

6. Check it at the same time each day

Tie the check to something that already happens, like right before dinner or right after school. That way it's a routine and not a nagging session.

7. Start fresh every week

A new, clean chart every Monday gives everyone a do-over. That's one reason printables work so well: print a new copy each week, or laminate one and use a dry-erase marker.

8. Change it when it gets stale

When kids get bored, swap a chore or change the reward. In summer, try a "before screens" list like the free Summer Chore Chart.
